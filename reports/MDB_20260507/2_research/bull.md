
Bull Analyst: I get why the bear camp is cautious on **MongoDB (MDB)**. The chart is still bruised, the stock isn’t cheap on traditional metrics, and SBC is high. But I think that misses the bigger point:

**the business has improved faster than the stock price has recognized.**

This is exactly the kind of setup I like on the long side: a premium software company that already got derated hard, while fundamentals are still compounding and profitability is inflecting.

## My bull case in one sentence

**MDB is a high-quality infrastructure software name with durable 20%+ revenue growth, a fortress balance sheet, rapidly improving cash flow, and a technical setup that suggests the washout may already be behind it.**

---

## First, let’s address the “this is just a broken chart” argument

Yes, technically, MDB is still below the **200-day SMA (317.67)**. That’s true. But that’s a trader’s objection, not a business-quality objection.

What matters is that the stock is no longer acting like it’s in free-fall:

- **Price: 265.26**
- Above the **10 EMA: 261.17**
- Above the **50 SMA: 261.76**
- **MACD: +0.43**, now back above zero
- **RSI: 53.10**, constructive and not overbought

So bears saying “the long-term trend is damaged” are right — but only partially. The more important question is whether the stock is transitioning from panic to accumulation. The answer is increasingly **yes**.

And frankly, if you wait until the chart looks perfect, you’re probably buying much closer to the old highs than the current reset zone.

## The business is still growing at a rate most large software companies would envy

This is where the bear case gets weakest.

### Revenue growth is not broken
- **FY2026 revenue: $2.464B**
- **FY2025 revenue: $2.006B**
- **YoY growth: +22.8%**

And the latest quarter was even stronger:

- **Q4 FY2026 revenue: $695.1M**
- Up from **$548.4M**
- **YoY growth: +26.7%**

That’s not a decelerating, exhausted software name. That’s a company still putting up elite growth at meaningful scale.

Even better, quarterly revenue progression is clean:

- **$549.0M**
- **$591.4M**
- **$628.3M**
- **$695.1M**

That’s sequential acceleration in absolute dollars. Bears love to talk about valuation, but valuation only truly becomes dangerous when growth is slipping. Here, the growth engine is still very much alive.

### Scalability is obvious
MDB is already at **$2.464B in revenue** with only **$5M in capex** for the year. That is an incredibly asset-light model.

When a software business can keep growing over 20% with:
- **71.8% gross margin**
- rising cash flow
- minimal capital intensity

...that is exactly the kind of operating model you want to own through an earnings inflection.

## The profitability story is improving materially — faster than bears admit

A common bear argument is: “Sure, revenue is growing, but real profits still aren’t there.”

That argument is outdated.

### Losses are narrowing sharply
- **Net loss FY2025: -$129.1M**
- **Net loss FY2026: -$71.2M**

- **Operating loss FY2025: -$216.1M**
- **Operating loss FY2026: -$137.0M**

That’s real operating leverage.

And in the latest quarter:
- **Operating income: +$0.3M**
- versus **-$18.6M** a year ago

Is that huge? No. But it matters. For a company at MDB’s scale, crossing from persistent operating losses toward breakeven is exactly how the next leg of re-rating starts.

### Cash flow inflection is the biggest clue
This is the part bears really struggle with.

- **Operating cash flow FY2026: $505.1M**
- up from **$150.2M** the prior year

- **Free cash flow FY2026: $500.2M**
- up from **$120.6M** the prior year

That is not cosmetic improvement. That is a major financial inflection.

Yes, SBC inflates cash flow relative to pure economic earnings. I’ll come back to that. But even after acknowledging that, a company generating this level of cash while still growing 20%+ is not one you dismiss lightly.

## The balance sheet is a huge hidden strength

This is where the bull case gets even stronger.

### MDB is basically overcapitalized
- **Cash + short-term investments: $2.387B**
- **Total debt: $32.9M**
- **Net cash: about $2.354B**

That balance sheet does three things for investors:

1. **Removes financial risk**
2. **Gives management flexibility** for buybacks, acquisitions, or continued investment
3. **Supports valuation** because you’re not buying a fragile company dependent on capital markets

If you adjust for net cash, the valuation is less scary than headline multiples suggest.

### EV-to-sales is more reasonable than P/S
- Market cap: **$23.58B**
- Less net cash: roughly **$2.35B**
- Enterprise value: about **$21.2B**

Against **$2.464B** of revenue, that’s about **8.6x EV/sales**, not 9.6x market-cap-to-sales.

For a software infrastructure company growing **23-27%**, moving toward profitability, with a fortress balance sheet and high gross margins, that is not absurd.

Expensive? Sure.  
Unreasonable? No.

## Deferred revenue says demand is still healthy

This is an underrated point.

- Total deferred revenue rose to about **$470.7M**
- up from roughly **$359.8M** the year before

That’s meaningful.

Deferred revenue growth matters because it supports:
- demand durability

- confidence that top-line strength is not just a one-quarter fluke

So when bears say, “What if growth starts fading?” the current balance of evidence says the opposite: visibility is improving, not deteriorating.

## MongoDB still has real competitive advantages

Let’s not overcomplicate this. MDB is not just “another software stock.”

It has several durable strengths:

### 1. Developer-oriented product positioning
MongoDB’s core platform is built around a developer-friendly database architecture. In infrastructure software, that matters. Products developers like tend to gain organic adoption and remain sticky once embedded.

### 2. Mission-critical category
Databases are not nice-to-have tools. They are core infrastructure. Once a platform is integrated into applications and workflows, switching is not trivial. That creates real customer stickiness.

### 3. Strong brand in software infrastructure
MDB has enough scale now that this is no longer a speculative niche story:
- **$2.464B revenue**
- **$1.768B gross profit**
- large institutional following
- strong place in infrastructure software

### 4. Financial muscle
A lot of software companies can say they’re growing. Far fewer can say they have:
- **$2.387B in cash**
- virtually no debt
- **$500M in FCF**
- and improving margins

That combination gives MDB a strategic advantage, especially if the macro gets tougher.

## Now let’s take the bear arguments head-on

## Bear point 1: “The stock is too expensive”

I hear this one a lot. But “expensive” without context is lazy analysis.

Yes:
- **Forward P/E: 41.6x**
- **P/S: ~9.6x**
- **P/B: 8.0x**

But this is a company with:
- **22.8% annual revenue growth**
- **26.7% quarterly growth**
- rapidly narrowing losses
- massive FCF inflection
- net cash balance sheet
- improving operating leverage

Premium software names should trade at premium multiples when the growth is real and the financial quality is improving.

And remember: the stock already corrected dramatically from the **444.72** high. A lot of valuation excess has already been wrung out by price compression while fundamentals kept improving.

That’s exactly why the bull setup is attractive now.

---

## Bear point 2: “SBC is too high, so the FCF isn’t real”

This is the most valid bear critique, and it deserves a real answer.

Yes:
- **SBC was $550.5M**
- about **22.3% of revenue**

That is high. No argument.

But the bearish version of this point goes too far.

First, the company is not ignoring dilution:
- **$400.3M of stock repurchases** in FY2026

Second, even if you haircut cash flow quality because of SBC, MDB still clearly moved from modest cash generation to substantial cash generation. The direction is undeniable.

Third, SBC problems are far more dangerous when:
- balance sheets are weak
- growth is slowing
- profitability is getting worse

MDB is the opposite:
- balance sheet is elite
- growth is strong
- profitability is improving

So yes, investors should monitor SBC. But SBC is not a thesis-breaker when the company is this liquid and this operationally strong.

---

## Bear point 3: “Net income is flattered by interest income”

Also true — but again, incomplete.

MDB benefited from:
- **$85.5M of net interest income** in FY2026

That helped narrow the net loss. Fine.

But the bull case does **not** depend on interest income.

The real story is:
- operating loss improved from **-$216.1M** to **-$137.0M**
- Q4 operating income turned slightly positive
- FCF surged
- revenue growth remained above 20%

In other words, interest income helped the optics, but the core operating business is still clearly improving on its own.

And if a company’s large cash balance produces interest income, that’s not a flaw. That’s a benefit of having a superior balance sheet.

---

## Bear point 4: “Gross margin is slipping”

Gross margin fell from **73.3% to 71.8%**.

Should we watch that? Absolutely.

But let’s keep perspective:
- **71.8% gross margin is still excellent**
- gross profit still rose to **$1.768B**
- revenue growth and cash generation more than offset that modest margin pressure

This is a watch item, not a reason to avoid the stock.

---

## Bear point 5: “Technically, it’s still a countertrend bounce”

Short-term? That’s fair.

But from an investing standpoint, this is often the sweet spot:
- panic washed out
- fundamentals are improving
- technicals are stabilizing
- sentiment is still cautious

If the stock closes above **269-273**, the next technical target is around **280-281**. And if momentum keeps improving, the market will start focusing less on the damaged 200-day and more on the operating inflection.

Investors make the best returns by buying when the chart is healing, not after it’s already healed.

The technical report lands on **HOLD**, and I understand why for short-term traders.

But for investors, I think that stance is too conservative.

Why?

Because the stock market usually turns **before** the long-term moving averages do. If you require full chart repair before buying, you’re effectively outsourcing your decision-making to lagging indicators.

Meanwhile, the business is already showing:
- sustained **20%+ growth**
- improving operating margins
- **$500M FCF**
- rising deferred revenue
- almost no debt
- huge net cash cushion

That is not a “wait forever” profile. That’s an **accumulate** profile.
