
Bear Analyst: I’ll push back on your bull case pretty directly: **MongoDB may be a better company than it was a year ago, but that does not automatically make MDB a good stock to buy here.** That’s the core mistake in the long thesis.

A lot of what you’re calling “improvement” is real. The problem is **the market is still being asked to pay a premium price for earnings quality that remains unproven, cash flow that is heavily SBC-driven, and a chart that still looks like a countertrend rally inside a broken structure**.

## 1) “The chart is healing” — yes, but that’s not the same as investable upside

You’re leaning hard on the fact that MDB is above the **10 EMA (261.17)** and **50 SMA (261.76)**, with **MACD at +0.43** and **RSI 53.10**.

Fine. That says the rebound is alive.

But the technical report’s main conclusion is the more important one: **this is a recovery rally after a major drawdown, not a repaired uptrend**.

Let’s be clear about what the market already told you:

- The stock collapsed from above **430-440**
- It gapped down on **2026-03-03** from **325.01 to 252.73**
- It washed out to **225.95** in April
- It is still well below the **200 SMA at 317.67**

That’s not just “a bruised chart.” That is a **structural reset in expectations**.

And buying here means buying **right into overhead resistance**:

- Resistance: **269-273**
- Next band: **280-281**
- Key support below: **255**, then **244**, then **227**

So the near-term upside from **265.26** to first resistance is maybe **3%**, while downside back to **244** is roughly **8%**, and to **227** is **14%+**. With **ATR at 14.11**, one normal volatility swing can wipe out your “accumulate here” idea fast.

So no — I don’t think “the chart is healing” is enough. I think it says **don’t chase a rebound that hasn’t cleared real resistance**.

## 2) “Growth is elite” — but it still isn’t converting cleanly into durable profits

You’re right that revenue growth is strong:

- **FY2026 revenue: $2.464B, +22.8% YoY**
- **Q4 revenue: $695.1M, +26.7% YoY**

That’s good. No bear should deny it.

But here’s the problem: **at nearly $2.5B of revenue, this business still posted a full-year operating loss of -$137.0M**.

And the “profitability inflection” you cite is a lot less impressive when you look closely:

- **Q4 operating income: just $0.3M**
- On **$695.1M** of revenue

That is basically **breakeven by rounding error**, not proof of a durable margin model.

Worse, the quality of those improving earnings is still questionable:

- **FY2026 operating loss:** **-$137.0M**
- **FY2026 pretax loss:** **-$55.7M**
- Difference partly explained by **$85.5M of net interest income**

That matters.

The same thing happened in Q4:

- **Operating income:** **$0.3M**
- **Net income:** **$15.5M**
- Supported by **$20.6M of net interest income**

So when you say “the core business is improving,” sure, modestly. But the numbers also say **core operations are still not really generating meaningful profits on their own**.

That’s a huge distinction for a stock trading at a premium multiple.

## 3) The cash flow story is much weaker than bulls present it

This is the biggest issue in your argument.

You keep pointing to:

- **Operating cash flow: $505.1M**
- **Free cash flow: $500.2M**

But the fundamental report also gives the key offset:

- **Stock-based compensation: $550.5M**

That means **SBC exceeded reported free cash flow**.

So when bulls call MDB a $500M FCF story, they’re skipping the part where that cash generation is heavily flattered by paying employees in stock instead of cash.

And the dilution is not theoretical. It is happening:

- **Basic average shares FY2025:** **74.56M**
- **Basic average shares FY2026:** **81.25M**

That’s roughly **9% share count growth in a single year**.

Now look at the supposed fix:

- **Buybacks in FY2026:** **$400.3M**

So management spent **$400M of cash**, and yet shareholders still ended up with meaningfully more shares outstanding on average.

That’s not a shareholder-friendly capital return story. That’s **cash being used to fight dilution it still isn’t containing**.

So I don’t buy the line that “SBC is a watch item, not a thesis-breaker.” At **22.3% of revenue**, it is absolutely a thesis-level issue. It means the economic value creation is much thinner than the reported cash flow suggests.

## 4) The balance sheet is strong — but bulls are overstating what that means for the stock

Yes, MDB has:

- **$2.387B** in cash and short-term investments
- **$32.9M** of debt
- Roughly **$2.354B net cash**

That reduces financial distress risk. Agreed.

But bulls turn that into “therefore the valuation is reasonable,” and that’s where the logic breaks.

Even on your preferred enterprise value framing:

- EV is about **$21.2B**
- Revenue is **$2.464B**
- EV/sales is still about **8.6x**

That is **not cheap** for a company that:

- still has **negative full-year GAAP operating income**
- saw **gross margin fall** from **73.3% to 71.8%**
- has **SBC above FCF**
- and is relying on interest income to help its earnings optics

Also, that cash pile isn’t “free upside.” If a big portion of future cash is needed to offset dilution and support comp-heavy growth, then the balance sheet is less of a hidden asset and more of a buffer against a business model that still hasn’t matured.

## 5) “Deferred revenue is rising” — okay, but that doesn’t solve the core problem

Deferred revenue rising to **~$470.7M** from **~$359.8M** is supportive. I’m not denying that.

But it’s not some trump card.

Deferred revenue tells you there is visibility. It does **not** tell you:

- margins will expand
- dilution will improve
- competition will ease
- or the stock deserves nearly **9x EV/sales**

This is exactly what I think the bull case keeps doing: taking a positive operating datapoint and acting like it answers the valuation and earnings-quality questions. It doesn’t.

## 6) On competition and moat: the economics are not as strong as the narrative

You describe MongoDB as a mission-critical, developer-loved infrastructure platform. Maybe. But if the moat were translating cleanly into economics, I’d expect to see:

- stronger operating margins at this scale
- lower SBC intensity
- better share count control
- and no gross margin slippage

Instead we have:

- **Gross margin down 150 bps**
- **SG&A still $1.188B**
- **R&D $716.3M**
- **Full-year operating loss still negative**
- **SBC still enormous**

That suggests MDB is still paying heavily to sustain growth. That doesn’t mean the product is weak. It means **the monetization quality and competitive economics are not yet strong enough to justify complacency**.

## 7) The biggest flaw in your thesis: you are paying today for a future that still has to be earned

This is the heart of it.

You’re asking investors to underwrite:

- sustained **20%+ growth**
- continued operating leverage
- lower dilution pressure
- stable or improving gross margins
- and a successful transition from near-breakeven to real earnings power

But the valuation already assumes a lot of that works.

The fundamental report literally says the market is paying for future margin expansion, not current earnings. Exactly. And that is why the downside remains real.

If growth slips from the low-20s into the mid-teens, or if margins stall, or if SBC stays elevated, **multiple compression can easily continue**. A stock being down from **444** does not make it cheap. Sometimes a stock is down because the old multiple was wrong.

## Bottom line

I’m not arguing MDB is a bad company. I’m arguing **it is still a risky stock to buy here**.

My bear case is simple:

- **Technically**, this is still a rebound inside a damaged long-term trend
- **Fundamentally**, core profitability is still weak and flattered by interest income
- **Cash flow quality** is compromised by **$550.5M of SBC**
- **Dilution remains real**, even after **$400.3M of buybacks**
- **Valuation is still premium** at roughly **8.6x EV/sales** and **41.6x forward P/E**
- **Risk/reward is poor near current levels** with resistance just overhead and volatility still high

So no, I would **not** “accumulate around current levels.”

If anything, the more disciplined stance is:

- **Hold at best if you already own it**
- **Do not chase this rebound**
- **Wait for either much better proof of core profitability or a meaningfully better entry**

The bull story is asking investors to trust the next leg of improvement.

The bear view is simpler: **after a major reset, with shaky earnings quality and dilution still running hot, trust should be earned — not assumed.**
